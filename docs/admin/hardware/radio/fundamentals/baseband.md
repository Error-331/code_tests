# Baseband 

Baseband in radio is the original, raw range of frequencies occupied by a signal—such as human voice or digital data—before it is modulated onto a higher-frequency 
carrier wave (it typically starts at or near zero hertz (DC)).

## Key Concepts

- **Unmodulated Signal**: pure message data, like audio from a microphone or binary data from a processor, without any high-frequency carrier shifts;
- **Frequency Range**: Spans from near 0 Hz up to a maximum limit defined by the data or sound bandwidth (e.g., 20 Hz to 20 kHz for voice);
- **Baseband Processor**: A dedicated chip in wireless devices (like phones) that handles the encoding, decoding, and processing of these signals before they are 
converted into radio frequencies (RF) for transmission through the air;
